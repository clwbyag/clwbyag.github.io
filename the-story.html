The Story

A connected investigation into identity, production and power at Chittaranjan Locomotive Works.

Long read

Steel, skill and a contested future

Chittaranjan Locomotive Works is more than a factory. It is a planned industrial township, a public-sector landmark, and for generations of workers a source of livelihood and identity in West Bengal’s railway belt.

This project follows a single narrative arc across formats: history establishes why CLW matters; reporting on production change examines what workers say is shifting on the shop floor; the privatisation debate separates rumour, allegation and evidence; interviews place human voices — labour and management — side by side; multimedia and data invite the audience to see patterns and absences in the public record.

Editorial standard: Privatisation is treated as a concern and contested claim unless supported by verified policy documents or official confirmation. Workers’ descriptions of assembly-based production are attributed as such. Official statements are presented in their own section for contrast.

Why this story now

Across Indian industry, public-sector manufacturing units face pressure to modernise, outsource and compete. At CLW, that pressure is felt in concrete ways: which components are made in-house, which arrive from private vendors, how work is organised, and how secure workers feel about the next decade of employment.

Union representatives argue that the plant’s character is changing — from a manufacturing hub toward an assembly-oriented unit — with implications for indigenous capability. Management messaging, where available, stresses production targets, technology upgrades and continuity under Indian Railways. The public is left to weigh incomplete information.

How the chapters connect

  1. History of CLW — establishment, electric locomotive legacy and indigenous manufacturing.
  2. The Changing Tracks — production process change and private-company involvement as described by sources.
  3. Privatisation debate — what is alleged, what is confirmed, what remains open.
  4. Voices from CLW — interviews with union leadership, management and workers.
  5. Multimedia — video, audio and photo documentary layers.
  6. Data story — production and workforce charts with placeholders until verified figures are added.
“The story of CLW is not only about machines. It is about who builds them, who owns the process, and who decides what ‘public’ manufacturing means in the twenty-first century.” Project framing note

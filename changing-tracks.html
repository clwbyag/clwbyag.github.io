The Changing Tracks

Manufacturing, assembly and the contested meaning of indigenous production at CLW.

Chapter 02

What is changing on the shop floor?

Workers and union representatives describe a structural shift: fewer processes completed entirely within CLW, more components and sub-assemblies arriving from outside, and a production rhythm that feels closer to assembly than to full locomotive manufacturing.

Attribution: Descriptions of “assembly-based production” in this section reflect workers’ and union accounts gathered for this project, unless independently verified through official production data. Official perspectives appear in Voices from CLW.

From complete manufacturing toward assembly — as described by workers

According to labour sources interviewed for this project, stages that once defined CLW’s craft identity — fabrication of key parts, deeper in-house machining, and tightly integrated electrical and mechanical work — have been reduced or reorganised. In their telling, locomotives increasingly arrive as kits of outsourced elements that are put together inside the plant.

If accurate, such a shift would not merely be a technical detail. It would alter skill requirements, bargaining power, and the claim that CLW remains a centre of indigenous heavy manufacturing.

“We used to make the locomotive. Now many say we are putting it together.” Worker account — see Voices for full context & audio

Involvement of external private companies

Union leaders allege that private firms play a growing role in supplying critical assemblies and participating in production workflows. The precise contractual arrangements — vendor lists, value of contracts, and degree of on-site private labour — require documentary verification. This website flags those as open questions pending purchase orders, RTI findings or official disclosures.

  • Claim (workers/unions): Private companies increasingly occupy space once held by departmental manufacturing.
  • Status: Reported as allegation / concern — not presented here as proven privatisation of CLW as an entity.
  • Needed evidence: Contract records, official circulars, production process manuals, employment statistics.

Indigenous manufacturing — an open future

Indigenous manufacturing is not only about the final locomotive badge. It is about retaining the ability to design, fabricate, test and repair complex systems without permanent dependence on external vendors. Workers fear that once skills atrophy, they rarely return. Whether CLW’s current trajectory constitutes healthy modernisation or a quiet erosion of public capacity is the core unresolved question of this project.

Employment and dignity

Concerns extend beyond process charts. Interviewees raise job security, redeployment, contractualisation and the social fabric of a township built around stable railway employment. These themes deepen in the dedicated labour story and interview archive.
